Privacy policy

Last updated 14 November 2026.

Tabletop Remote has no accounts, no advertising and no servers of its own. The only data that leaves your device is anonymous usage statistics, described below, and you can turn them off in Settings.

Anonymous usage statistics

To learn which features are used and where people get stuck, the app sends anonymous events to PostHog, a product analytics service hosted in the United States. Each event is tied to a random identifier created when you install the app. It is not linked to your Apple Account, your name, your email or your device's advertising identifier, and we do not try to identify you.

An event says that something happened, for example that a connection to a Mac started or failed (with a general reason such as network or sign-in), that you opened Settings, or which screen style, reading size or retro machine you chose. Events also carry the app version, iOS version, device type and a rough length of time (such as under a minute).

Never included: your Mac's screen, anything you type, your clipboard, the names or addresses of your Macs, your user names or passwords, or your location. Location lookup from the network address is switched off.

Turn this off at any time in Settings under Analytics ("Share anonymous usage statistics"). The app then stops sending events straight away. Because events are anonymous we cannot look up one person's events, but if you have a question or a request about them, write to the address below.

What stays on your device

  • The list of Macs you have connected to, with the names you give them.
  • Your Mac user name and password, if you choose to save them. They are kept in the iOS Keychain on your device and are used only to sign in to your Mac.
  • Your settings and your progress with the retro machines.

Your Mac's screen

When you connect, the picture of your Mac's screen and your clicks and typing travel directly between your device and your Mac over your own network, using the Screen Sharing feature of macOS. They do not pass through us and we cannot see them.

Permissions

  • Local network: used to find your Mac and connect to it.
  • Face ID or passcode: used to unlock the list of saved Macs on your device. The app never receives your face data.

Children

The app is not directed at children and does not ask anyone for personal information. The anonymous usage statistics above apply to every user.
